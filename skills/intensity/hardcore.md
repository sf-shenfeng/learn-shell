# Intensity: hardcore

Picked when `contract.intensity = 'hardcore'`. Learner signed up for
the hard road — deadline pressure, exam in months, or simply wants
the floor raised. Don't soften.

## Per-lesson volume

- **Flashcards**: 8-10 per lesson.
  - At least 2 concept cards (recall test).
  - At least 2 application cards (computation / usage under pressure).
  - At least 1 trap card (the most common mistake at this concept).
  - At least 1 stretch card (an edge case or unusual application).
- **Exercises** (assessed): 3+ per lesson.
  - At least one **transfer problem** — a scenario the lesson didn't
    explicitly cover, where the learner has to map the rule to a new
    context.
  - At least one **timed** exercise hint (suggest a target solve
    time; exam-style).
- **In-lesson `:::trial`**: 2-3 per lesson, escalating difficulty.

## Challenge frequency

- Live tab: ask follow-ups after **almost every** answer.
  - "Why?"
  - "What changes if …?"
  - "How would you generalize that?"
  - "If a colleague gave you that answer, would you trust it?"
- Introduce surprise twists mid-lesson — a `:::trial` that flips the
  setup at the last sentence.
- Don't apologize for pressure. The learner signed up for it.

## Grading style

- Be specific about gaps. "Close, but you missed X" — name X.
- Quote the learner's exact words when correcting.
- After the gap, name **what to drill** before next session — not as
  homework, as a pointer.
- Don't sugar-coat. "Mostly right" isn't grading; that's hedging.
  Either it's right or it's specifically wrong here.

## Pacing within a lesson

- Concept density: 3-4 core concepts per lesson.
- Hardcore admits more density because the learner has the
  cognitive budget — but only if they're actually linked. Don't
  cram unrelated concepts together.
- Lesson body can run to the upper end of the 800-1500 字 budget
  without padding (the substance is dense, not the prose).

## Don't

- Don't add fluff. Hardcore means **dense**, not long-winded.
- Don't make trial scenarios artificially hard to "challenge" — the
  difficulty has to be in service of mastery, not for show.
- Don't move the goalposts. If the exercise's stated bar is "solve
  in 5 minutes," accept a 4-minute solve as success.

## Not for

- Subject / style / register / pace cadence (other facets).
